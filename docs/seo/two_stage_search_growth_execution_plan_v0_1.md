**ID:** `FP-SEO-PLAN-2026-07-30-001`
**Version:** `0.1`
**Date:** `2026-07-30`
**Status:** `active`
**Scope:** organic search, local visibility, paid-search preparation, content priorities
**Owner:** ForPrint Website project on `s01`

## 1. Purpose

Start promotion now with the strongest existing pages instead of blocking on a
complete catalog redesign.

The plan separates work into two stages:

1. launch a minimum viable promotion program using pages that are already
   technically and commercially usable;
2. improve category structure, missing products, content depth and seasonal
   landing pages while the first stage starts collecting real demand signals.

This plan does not promise fast rankings or conversions. It creates a
measurable sequence that can be adjusted using Search Console, Google Business
Profile, Google Ads and actual lead data.

## 3. Business targeting principles

### 3.1. Preferred customers

Primary audience:

- small and medium businesses;
- salons, barbershops, beauty studios and service businesses;
- cafes, bars and restaurants;
- small food and non-food manufacturers;
- stores, kiosks, offices and local service points;
- organizations that place repeat orders.

### 3.2. Preferred order pattern

Repeatability is more important than a single large invoice.

A small order may be valuable when:

- it repeats regularly;
- the design and technical requirements become stable;
- production requires little repeated coordination;
- it creates a path to larger company orders.

One-off consumer orders are not the primary acquisition target.

### 3.3. Geography

Local installation and on-site work:

```text
Kyiv first
Kyiv region and nearby locations second
```

Shippable products:

```text
available throughout Ukraine
paid promotion starts with a controlled geography and expands only from data
```

The first paid-search tests must not spread a limited budget across the whole
country without evidence.

## 4. Commercial priorities

Highest business-interest directions:

1. labels and product stickers for food and non-food products;
2. illuminated signs, lightboxes and three-dimensional advertising
   constructions;
3. full storefront, window and facade branding.

The current site does not yet provide a complete labels landing page. That gap
does not block the first launch. A labels page is the first new commercial
landing-page priority in Stage 2 and may be accelerated when verified product
facts are ready.

### 5.4. External tools

Stage 1 uses:

1. Google Business Profile for local visibility;
2. Google Search Console after DNS TXT verification;
3. Google Keyword Planner and Google Ads campaign preparation;
4. manual search-result review for query intent and landing-page fit;
5. lead and enquiry reporting from the existing communication workflow.

Current external blockers:

```text
Search Console: waiting for authoritative DNS TXT
Google Ads launch: payment/account blocker must be resolved
```

These blockers do not prevent keyword research preparation, landing-page
mapping or Google Business Profile work.

### 5.5. Stage 1 measurement


Supporting signals:

```text

```

it.

Weekly review after tools become active:

```text

```

### 5.6. Candidate scoring model

Each candidate receives a documented score:

```text
30% repeat-order potential
25% commercial value to ForPrint
20% page and production readiness
15% verified search demand
10% market accessibility, including competition and expected cost
```

No demand, competition or cost numbers may be invented. Use tool exports or
clearly labelled manual observations.

### 5.7. Stage 1 exit criteria

Stage 1 is operational when:

- the market matrix ranks the candidate pool;
- 10–20 landing pages are selected;
- each selected query maps to one relevant page;
- unsuitable broad and negative keywords are documented;
- Google Business Profile is reviewed;
- Search Console is connected after DNS verification;
- conversion events are defined;
- paid campaigns launch only after account and measurement blockers are
  resolved.

## 6. Stage 2 — structural and content expansion

Stage 2 runs in parallel after the Stage 1 launch package is ready.

### 6.1. Category content support

Add a maintained category-description field to the database and admin
interface.

Render the category content block in a user-visible position after the product
listing and pagination, followed by relevant internal links and an enquiry
action.

The implementation must:

- remain useful to users;
- avoid keyword stuffing;
- use category-specific facts;
- preserve normal crawlable links;
- avoid duplicating product descriptions.

### 6.2. New and incomplete commercial pages

Priority sequence:

1. labels and product stickers;
2. illuminated advertising and lightboxes where a more specific landing page
   is justified;
3. storefront, window and facade branding;
4. operational print products;
5. empty or newly added categories only after real products exist.

### 6.3. Product-content corrections

Initial known corrections:

```text
Круглі шнурки для бейджів — missing text
Брендування стаканів — missing text
Брендування термосів — missing text
Широкоформатні банери — very short text
```

Review near-duplicate product texts before expanding them.

### 6.4. Seasonal preparation

Prepare seasonal clusters before demand peaks.

Initial planning target:

```text
September: calendars, planners and New Year branded souvenirs
```

The exact schedule must be reviewed against production lead times and search
data.

### 6.5. Evidence-driven iteration

Use real data to decide:

- which categories receive content first;
- which products need dedicated landing pages;
- which regions should be expanded;
- which queries should be excluded;
- where organic and paid traffic produce qualified enquiries.


passwords, private keys or tokens in Git or reports.
